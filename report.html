Executive Summary

Deliverable 1 · Revised report

The 2026 State of the Nation Address (27 July 2026) reported broad program delivery during a year shaped by the Middle East conflict and a national energy emergency. Official data released since the address change the picture in important ways. Poverty has fallen faster than planned. But overall SDG progress remains too slow, health and disaster-related goals are going backwards, and price pressures are rising again. Five findings matter most for the House.

1. Poverty fell to a record low of 9.7% in 2025, ahead of the national plan, but price shocks now put the gains at risk.

The PSA reports that poverty incidence among Filipinos fell from 15.5% in 2023 to 9.7% in 2025, about 11 million people, and that poverty fell in every region. The Philippine Development Plan targets 8–9% by 2028. However, headline inflation reached 7.2% in September 2026, with rice at 20.3% and inflation for the poorest 30% of households at 9.0%. The 2025 figures refer to incomes in 2025, before the 2026 energy shock.

Why it matters to Congress: The FY2027 budget will determine whether social protection keeps pace with prices. Gains measured before the shock should not be read as permanent.

2. Only 2 of 17 SDGs are moving fast enough to meet 2030 targets; health (SDG 3) and sustainable cities (SDG 11) are regressing.

The PSA’s latest SDG Pace of Progress (data as of 7 May 2026) shows 10 of 17 goals improving since 2015. Only SDG 12 and SDG 14 are on pace. Of the 99 indicators with enough data, 23 are on track, 51 need faster progress and 25 are regressing. Five goals (SDG 5, 10, 13, 16, 17) lack enough data to assess.

Why it matters to Congress: With four years left, most targets need faster progress, not continuity. The data gaps also limit oversight on gender, inequality, climate and governance.

3. Child nutrition and learning remain the deepest human-capital gaps, and stunting has risen for the first time in a decade.

Stunting among children under five rose to 25.3% in 2025 from 23.6% in 2023 (DOST-FNRI). About one in three Filipinos (32.6%) experience moderate or severe food insecurity. In PISA 2022, only 16% of 15-year-olds reached minimum proficiency in mathematics and 24% in reading. The classroom backlog is 143,543 according to DepEd (June 2026), or more than 166,000 according to EDCOM 2.

Why it matters to Congress: These outcomes shape workforce productivity for decades. The conflicting classroom and nutrition figures also show that oversight depends on reconciling agency data.

4. The House is already acting on the address’s legislative requests; fiscal and design details remain open.

House Bill 10345, which raises the income-tax exemption from ₱250,000 to ₱350,000, was approved by the Committee on Ways and Means on 10 August 2026. That committee also approved exempting microenterprises from the minimum corporate income tax. VAT on allowable system-loss charges was removed administratively in August 2026 (ERC Resolution No. 26; BIR RMC 97-2026), while bills to amend EPIRA remain pending. No public estimate of revenue forgone accompanied the requests.

Why it matters to Congress: Revenue measures originate in the House (Const. Art. VI, §24). Lawmakers will need a fiscal-impact estimate and a clear distribution of benefits to weigh these bills against the ₱7.2-trillion 2027 spending program.

5. Accountability for flood-control projects is advancing, but the headline recovery figure is not yet independently documented.

The address reported nearly ₱25 billion in money and assets recovered, frozen or preserved, and more than ₱800 million returned to the Treasury. Independently reported milestones include the Independent Commission for Infrastructure’s referral of 65 individuals to the Ombudsman before it concluded in March 2026, ₱611 million in restitution turned over to the DOJ, and cases filed before the Sandiganbayan. The Philippines again ranks first of 193 countries on the WorldRiskIndex 2025.

Why it matters to Congress: Flood-control integrity directly affects whether communities in the world’s highest-risk country are protected. A consolidated, auditable recovery report is a reasonable oversight request.

Current Situation and Verified Evidence

Context for the 2027 budget cycle

  • Energy shock: The government declared a national energy emergency in March 2026 (Executive Order No. 110) after the Strait of Hormuz closure disrupted Gulf crude supply, on which the country relies heavily.
  • Prices: Inflation re-accelerated to 7.2% in September 2026, above the 2–4% target range. The year-to-date average is 5.4%.
  • Jobs: Unemployment was 4.9% in June 2026 (4.8% in May); 6.1 million employed Filipinos were underemployed.
  • Budget: The 2027 National Expenditure Program totals ₱7.2 trillion (21.7% of GDP), up 5.99% from 2026. It was transmitted to Congress on 11 August 2026.
  • Bangsamoro: The first BARMM parliamentary elections were held on 14 September 2026 under RA 12317. No party won a majority of the 40 party-list seats.

Development Gaps and Legislative Implications

Each priority area follows the same sequence: current situation, verified evidence, the gap, who is most affected, the consequences of inaction, and what falls within Congress’s mandate.

A. Protecting poverty gains during a price shock (SDG 1, 2)

Situation and evidence. Poverty fell to 9.7% in 2025, but 2026 inflation is running well above target, led by rice and transport. DSWD reports more than 2.8 million active 4Ps households against an annual target of about 4.4 million, with 400,000–500,000 validated households awaiting enrolment (August 2026). The ₱60-billion crisis-relief allocation announced in the SONA runs only to year-end.

Most affected. Low-income households, whose inflation rate reached 9.0% in September 2026, and regions with the highest poverty, such as Bicol (16.8%).

Implications of inaction. Households that recently moved just above the poverty line are the most likely to fall back if relief ends while prices remain high.

Within Congress’s mandate. Appropriations for 4Ps enrolment of the validated waitlist under RA 11310; oversight of the targeting and results of crisis relief; and a reporting requirement in the General Appropriations Act on beneficiaries reached.

B. Child nutrition and foundational learning (SDG 2, 4)

Situation and evidence. Stunting rose to 25.3% in 2025. Rural children and those in BARMM are most affected. The National Nutrition Council’s Operation Timbang Plus reported about 5% stunting, against FNRI’s survey estimate of 25.3%, a gap flagged by EDCOM 2. Learning outcomes remain low, and the classroom backlog is disputed between DepEd and EDCOM 2.

Implications of inaction. Stunting and low learning permanently reduce earnings and productivity. Unreconciled data weakens budget targeting.

Within Congress’s mandate. Oversight of the First 1,000 Days law (RA 11148) and of school-building programs; direction to reconcile nutrition and classroom data before allocation; and use of EDCOM 2 (created under RA 11899) findings in budget deliberations.

C. Health financing and emerging health risks (SDG 3, regressing)

Situation and evidence. Out-of-pocket spending remains about 41% of current health spending (2025). Zero-balance billing covers ward care in the 87 DOH-run hospitals. HIV cases continue to rise among young people. The address also highlighted new PhilHealth benefits planned for 2027.

Implications of inaction. High out-of-pocket costs push households into poverty after illness and undercut the poverty gains in Area A.

Within Congress’s mandate. Oversight of Universal Health Care Act (RA 11223) implementation and PhilHealth benefit utilization; funding and oversight of the HIV and AIDS Policy Act (RA 11166), with a focus on youth prevention.

D. Disaster resilience and infrastructure integrity (SDG 11 regressing; SDG 13)

Situation and evidence. The Philippines tops the WorldRiskIndex 2025. The SONA reported a new network of disaster-response hubs and a target of relief delivery within 24 hours. That target has not yet been tested in a major event. Flood-control investigations are moving to the Ombudsman and the courts. SDG 13 lacks enough indicators for a PSA pace assessment, and 72.29 percentage points of the 75% NDC target are conditional on external finance.

Implications of inaction. Spending on defective or ghost flood-control projects leaves communities exposed and erodes public trust in infrastructure budgets.

Within Congress’s mandate. A consolidated recovery and case-status report from the Ombudsman, DOJ and DPWH; public, project-level disclosure conditions for flood-control appropriations; review of the proposed National Building Code (PD 1096) amendment; and oversight of the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act (RA 10121).

E. Energy affordability and transition (SDG 7)

Situation and evidence. VAT on allowable system losses was removed administratively in August 2026, prospectively. EPIRA amendment bills (HB 10273, HB 10306) remain pending, and the House has said it intends to act before the December recess. Electrification reached 94.77% (June 2025), with Mindanao at 83.81%. DOE estimates ₱80.9 billion is needed for full electrification by 2028.

Implications of inaction. High power costs compound inflation, and the households still without electricity are concentrated in poorer areas.

Within Congress’s mandate. Amend EPIRA (RA 9136) to define recoverable technical losses and settle the refund question; fund the electrification program; and oversee progress toward the 35% renewable target.

F. Evidence for SDG oversight (all goals)

Situation and evidence. Only 99 of 162 monitored SDG indicators have enough data to assess, and five goals cannot be assessed at all. The address did not name the SDG framework and gave no baselines or targets. The Philippines’ 2025 Voluntary National Review reported to the UN on SDGs 3, 5, 8, 14 and 17.

Within Congress’s mandate. Adequate appropriations for PSA data collection, and a request through the Committee on Sustainable Development Goals for agencies to report the indicators within each committee’s jurisdiction. The latter is a coordination step, not new legislation.

Recommended Actions

Within the constitutional powers of Congress
Constitutional limits. Congress may not increase the appropriations recommended by the President for the operation of government (Art. Implementation remains an executive function. Congress acts through legislation, appropriations and oversight.

Legislative Action Summary

Deliverable 3

Five priorities, ranked by consequence, strength of evidence and the directness of Congress’s role.

1

Protect the poverty gains in the FY2027 budget

Finding
Poverty fell to 9.7% (2025), but inflation is 7.2%, with rice at 20.3% (Sept 2026), and 400,000–500,000 validated households await 4Ps enrolment.
Implication
Gains measured before the 2026 energy shock may reverse for households just above the line.
Action
Fund waitlist enrolment; require quarterly reach and disbursement reporting for crisis relief.
Instrument
FY2027 GAA · Appropriations, Social Services
2

Decide the tax and power-cost measures on full fiscal information

Finding
HB 10345 (₱350K exemption) and the microenterprise MCIT exemption have cleared committee; EPIRA bills are pending; VAT on system losses is already removed administratively.
Implication
Relief to households is likely, but revenue effects and refund questions are unresolved.
Action
Obtain DOF revenue and distribution estimates; legislate technical-loss definitions and the refund question in the EPIRA amendment.
Instrument
Legislation · Ways and Means, Energy
3

Reverse the decline in child nutrition and close the classroom data gap

Finding
Stunting is up to 25.3%; administrative and survey estimates differ fivefold; the classroom backlog estimates differ by ~23,000.
Implication
Money cannot be targeted well without reconciled data; human-capital losses are long-lasting.
Action
Condition school-building and nutrition funds on reconciled figures; oversight of RA 11148.
Instrument
Oversight, budget conditions · Basic Education, Health, Appropriations
4

Make flood-control accountability auditable

Finding
The ₱25B recovery figure is government-reported; independent records show partial restitution and pending cases. The Philippines is the world’s highest-risk country.
Implication
Public trust in infrastructure spending and community protection depend on verifiable results.
Action
Request a consolidated recovery and case-status report; attach disclosure conditions to flood-control appropriations.
Instrument
Oversight, GAA conditions · Good Government, Public Accounts, Public Works
5

Give the House a single SDG scorecard

Finding
Only 2 of 17 goals are on pace; SDG 3 and 11 are regressing; 5 goals cannot be assessed for lack of data.
Implication
Without consistent data, Congress cannot tell which programs move SDG outcomes.
Action
Lead a cross-committee scorecard built on PSA indicators; support PSA data funding.
Instrument
Committee coordination, appropriations · Sustainable Development Goals (lead)

Method and Limitations

  • Claims were checked against official releases (PSA, DBM, DOE, DSWD, DOST-FNRI, OECD) and against reputable news reports of those releases, current to 11 October 2026.
  • This review was conducted using web search results; direct access to some primary portals (including psa.gov.ph) was unavailable. Figures cited through news reports are identified as such and should be confirmed against the primary release before formal citation.
  • The 2025 poverty threshold has been contested by some civil-society groups (e.g., IBON). Self-rated poverty (SWS) measures a different concept and is not comparable with official poverty statistics.
  • Regional figures from national surveys carry larger margins of error than national figures.
  • “Unverified” means no independent source was found. It does not mean the claim is false.
